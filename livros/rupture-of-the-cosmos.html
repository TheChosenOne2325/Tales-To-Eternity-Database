Monster Book

Rupture Of The Cosmos

Source: ROC

Tales To Eternity - Rupture Of The Cosmos

This is the Monster Book. In it, you will find the rules to create monsters and build encounters, and a bestiary of creatures ready to use at any level.

Chapter 1 — Creating Monsters

Concept, Level, and Role

Every monster is made of three parts:

  • Concept: What makes the monster unique: its type, size, Attributes, senses, languages, affinities, and abilities. A Wolf, a Goblin, and a Skeleton are concepts.
  • Level: From 1 to 12, on the same scale as the characters. The level sets the monster's numbers.
  • Role: Minion, Standard, Elite, or Boss. The role changes the monster's Life, attacks, and abilities.

Every concept in this book has a suggested level and role, but it works at any of them. The same Wolf can be a level 1 Minion or a level 9 Elite without rewriting anything.

To make a variant, change its equipment or abilities. A Goblin with a sword and a Goblin with spells are still the same concept.

Monsters Play Like Characters

  • Attacks: d20 + Attribute against Defense, just like a character.
  • Ability Difficulty: 10 + Attribute, just like a Spell.
  • Defense: From armor or natural protection (hide, scales, shell), using the same table as the characters.
  • Damage: From the weapon damage table, rising with tier. A monster wielding a weapon uses that weapon's damage.
  • Tests and Skills: The same rules as the characters.

  • Minion: Weak enemies that fall in a couple of hits, worth half a Standard. Minions of the same kind can all act on the same Initiative.
  • Standard: The common enemy, an even match for one character.
  • Elite: A stronger enemy, worth two Standards. Its second attack uses its Minor Action.
  • Boss: The main enemy of a story, made to face the whole group.

Bosses

Boss Actions: A Boss has 2 Boss Actions per round. It uses them at the end of a character's turn, one at a time, and recovers them at the start of its own turn. Without them, a single Boss would act once while the group acts four times.

Breaking the Rhythm: When a Boss would gain a Condition that takes away its actions (like Stunned or Incapacitated), it doesn't gain it. Instead, it loses its Boss Actions until the end of the next round.

Phases (optional): When the Boss becomes Injured, it gains a new ability or changes one of its abilities.

Abilities

Abilities come from the list in Chapter 2. Any monster can have any ability that fits its concept. Adding abilities is also how you make a monster stronger.

The damage of an ability follows the weapon damage table:

Type Base Category
Attack, single target Strong
Test, single target Medium
Small area (up to 2 Zones) Medium
Large area (3+ Zones) Weak
  • −1 category if it also causes a Condition or pushes.
  • +1 category if it can only be used once per combat.
  • A creature that succeeds on the Test takes half damage and doesn't suffer the Condition.

Affinities

Resistance, Vulnerability, and Immunity to damage types, and immunity to Conditions, are part of the concept and always apply. Abilities that negate or reduce damage are allowed, but they must be limited (usually once per combat), never at will.

Power

Power tells you how much a monster is worth in an encounter. It isn't exact, but it gives you a good idea when you change a monster. It compares the monster with a Standard Medium monster of the group's level:

  1. Toughness (T): The monster's Life ÷ the Standard Medium Life. Add 8% for each point of Defense above the reference value, or subtract 8% for each point below. Resistance to one or two of the weapon damage types (Blunt, Cut, Thrust) adds 25%, and to all three adds 50%. Regeneration or Undying adds 10% each.
  2. Threat (H): The monster's average damage per round ÷ the average of one Medium attack of its tier. Add 8% for each point of attack above the reference value, or subtract 8% for each point below. An ability used once per combat counts as ⅓ of its damage per round; an area ability counts as two targets.
  3. Points = 8 × √(T × H). Minions multiply the result by 0.75.

A Standard is worth 8 points, an Elite 16, and a Boss facing 4 characters about 32. Raising an Attribute or giving a monster a better weapon raises its points a little, but it doesn't change its role.

Building Encounters

An encounter has a point budget based on its difficulty and the number of characters:

Difficulty Points per Character Group of 4
Easy 4 16
Normal 6 24
Hard 8 32
Deadly 12 48

Spend the budget on monsters of the group's level. A Normal encounter is a balanced fight; a Hard one is a real challenge; a Deadly one can kill characters, especially in Tier 1.

Difficulty Examples for 4 Characters
Easy (16) 2 Standards · 1 Elite · 1 Standard + 2 Minions · 4 Minions
Normal (24) 3 Standards · 1 Elite + 1 Standard · 2 Standards + 2 Minions · 6 Minions
Hard (32) 1 Boss · 1 Elite + 2 Standards · 2 Elites
Deadly (48) 1 Boss + 2 Standards · 1 Boss + 4 Minions

For a Boss with an escort in a Hard encounter, use a Boss of a lower level with a few Minions.

Experience

A Normal encounter gives XP equal to (the group's next level × 100) ÷ 4, for each character. An Easy encounter gives half, a Hard one gives 1.5 times, and a Deadly one gives twice as much.

Monster Types

  • Aberration: Creatures from the rifts of the cosmos, alien to the world.
  • Beast: Natural animals, ordinary or giant.
  • Construct: Objects given motion by magic.
  • Dragon: Winged reptiles of great power, and their lesser kin.
  • Elemental: Living fire, frost, and raw mana.
  • Fiend: Malicious beings born of corruption.
  • Giant: Huge humanoid creatures.
  • Humanoid: People of every race.
  • Monstrosity: Unnatural creatures that mix beasts and magic.
  • Ooze: Shapeless, creeping masses.
  • Plant: Living, hostile vegetation.
  • Undead: The dead, raised by magic or curse.

Chapter 2 — Abilities

Abilities can be given to any monster that fits them. Values marked A > B > C > D change with the monster's tier. The Difficulty of an ability is 10 + the monster's Attribute.

Traits

Traits are passive and always active.

  • Amphibious: It can breathe air and water.
  • Aura (Damage Type): A creature that starts its turn adjacent to it takes Weak damage of that type.
  • Flight: It can fly with its Speed.
  • Keen Senses: It has Advantage on Perception Tests.
  • Pack Tactics: Its attacks have Advantage against a creature adjacent to one of its allies.
  • Regeneration: At the start of its turn, it regains Weak Life. Choose a damage type: if it took damage of that type since its last turn, it doesn't regenerate.
  • Sunlight Weakness: In bright sunlight, its attacks and Tests have Disadvantage.
  • Undying: The first time each combat it would drop to 0 Life, it drops to 1 instead, unless the damage was Heat or a critical hit.

Actions

  • Breath (once per combat): A Cone of 3 Zones. Each creature in it makes an Agility Test. On a failure, it takes Medium damage of the monster's type.
  • Charge: If it moves at least 3 Zones in a straight line before attacking, the target must make a Strength Test or fall Prone.
  • Drain: Make an attack. On a hit, it deals Medium damage and the monster regains Life equal to half the damage dealt.
  • Frightful Presence (once per combat): Each creature within 3 Zones that can see it makes a Spirit Test or becomes Scared until the end of its next turn.
  • Grab: Make an attack. On a hit, it deals Weak damage and the target becomes Restrained until it uses its Main Action to make a Strength or Agility Test to escape.
  • Petrifying Gaze (once per combat): A creature within 6 Zones makes a Spirit Test or becomes Restrained. It repeats the Test at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success.
  • Poison: When it hits with an attack, the target makes a Strength Test or takes Weak extra damage at the end of each of its turns, repeating the Test at the end of each turn.
  • Spellcasting: It knows 2 Spells from Fragments Of The Cosmos and can Conjure each one twice per combat, without spending Mana, at its own tier.
  • Swallow: Make an attack against a creature at least one size smaller. On a hit, it deals Medium damage and the target is swallowed: it is Restrained, can only attack the monster, and takes Weak damage at the start of each of its turns. It escapes if it deals damage equal to a quarter of the monster's maximum Life in one turn.
  • Web (once per combat): A Square of 2 Zones within 6 Zones. Each creature in it makes an Agility Test or becomes Restrained, repeating the Test at the end of each of its turns.

Reactions

  • Parry (once per combat): When it's hit by a melee attack, it reduces the damage by half.
  • Retaliate: When a creature adjacent to it hits it with a melee attack, it makes an attack against that creature.
  • Spiteful Burst: When it drops to 0 Life, each creature adjacent to it takes Weak damage of the monster's type.

Boss Actions

A Boss chooses the Boss Actions that fit it. Each one costs 1 Boss Action unless it says otherwise.

  • Attack: It makes one attack.
  • Command: An ally it can see moves up to its Speed or makes one attack, using its Reaction.
  • Move: It moves up to half its Speed without provoking Opportunity Attacks.
  • Roar (once per combat): Each enemy within 3 Zones makes a Spirit Test or becomes Scared until the end of the Boss's next turn.
  • Shake Off (2 actions): It ends one Condition on itself.
  • Signature (2 actions): It uses one of its abilities that isn't limited to once per combat.

Chapter 3 — Bestiary

Every monster here works at any level and in any role. Each one shows a suggested level and role, but you choose both when you use it.

Reading a Monster

  • Attacks: d20 + the Attribute shown in parentheses. You can swap it for another Attribute that fits, such as Strength for a monster with a heavy weapon.

  • Difficulty: 10 + the Attribute shown. "Difficulty Agility" means 10 + the monster's Agility.

  • Level: Choose from 1 to 12. Values written as A > B > C > D (Attributes, Defense, attacks, Difficulty, and damage) use the column of the monster's tier.

  • Life: Look up its Life category and level in the table in Chapter 1, then apply its role.

  • Role: Choose Minion, Standard, Elite, or Boss.

  • Skills: The rank shown is the one at its suggested level. Like a character, a monster is at most Trained in Tier 1, Expert in Tier 2, and Master from Tier 3 on.

Role Life Attacks per Turn Abilities
Minion Table ÷ 2 1, with Fixed damage (3 > 4 > 5 > 6) None
Standard Table 1 1
Elite Table × 2 2 (the second uses its Minor Action) 2
Boss Table × number of characters 2 + 2 Boss Actions 3

Each monster lists the abilities that fit its suggested role. If you change the role, remove abilities or add new ones from Chapter 2. A monster that becomes a Boss also chooses Boss Actions from Chapter 2.

Example: A Wolf at level 5 (Tier 2) as an Elite has 34 Life (Weak 17 × 2), Defense 12, attacks at +3, and makes two Bite attacks per turn, each dealing Medium (d8) Thrust damage.
