Player's Book

Fragments Of The Cosmos

Source: FTC

Tales To Eternity - Fragments Of The Cosmos

This first book is the Player's Book. In it, you will find all the information you need to play, while Game Masters will have two other books of their own.

Chapter 1 — Core Rules

Dice

The main die is the d20, although TTE uses all of the other common dice as well.

Advantage and Disadvantage

You roll a d6 and add it to your roll when you have advantage, or subtract it when you have disadvantage. Advantage and disadvantage don't stack, but they cancel each other out. The d6 never counts toward a natural 20.

Critical Rolls

Rolling a natural 1 or a natural 20 is a critical roll. A natural 1 automatically fails any test. A natural 20 automatically succeeds, as long as the task is possible, and on an attack it is a critical hit. Beyond the failure or success itself, a critical roll can also bring extra drawbacks or benefits.

Rounding

Always round up.

Specific Rules

Specific rules override general rules.

Difficulty

Every test has a Difficulty. To succeed, you must equal or beat it (this applies to attacks too).

The following baseline difficulties can be used for tests:

Difficulty DC
Easy 8
Normal 11
Hard 14
Very Hard 17
Nearly Impossible 20

Chapter 2 — Character

Attributes

When you create your character, distribute the values 2, 1, 1, 0, 0, and -1 among the attributes below. Attributes range from -5 to 5.

  • Strength: Raw physical power. It measures how hard you hit, how much you can lift and carry, and how well you can push, pull, or break things.
  • Agility: Speed and reflexes. It measures how quickly you move, react, and dodge danger.
  • Dexterity: Precision and fine motor control. It measures your aim and how skillfully you handle weapons, tools, and delicate tasks.
  • Intellect: Reasoning, memory, and knowledge. It measures how well you learn, analyze, and solve problems.
  • Spirit: Willpower and inner strength. It measures your resolve, your connection to mystical forces, and your resistance to fear and mental influence.
  • Charisma: Force of personality. It measures how well you persuade, lead, inspire, and deceive others.

Skills

Skills represent specialized knowledge in a particular area. They are not tied to attributes, and they have four ranks: Untrained (+0), Trained (+1), Expert (+2), and Master (+3).

If you gain the same skill from different sources, you can either choose a different skill instead or raise the rank of that skill, as long as you respect the tier limit:

  • Trained: Tier 1
  • Expert: Tier 2
  • Master: Tier 3

Skill List

  • Acrobatics: Keeping your balance, tumbling, flipping, and landing safely from falls.
  • Coordination: Performing tasks that demand precise, synchronized movements, such as juggling, catching, or doing two things at once.
  • Deception: Lying convincingly, disguising your intentions, and misleading others.
  • Gymnastics: Climbing, jumping, swimming, and other feats of physical exertion.
  • Insight: Reading people, sensing their true intentions, and noticing when someone is lying.
  • Intimidation: Influencing others through threats, fear, or a show of force.
  • Investigation: Searching for clues, analyzing evidence, and drawing conclusions.
  • Lore: Knowledge of history, legends, ancient cultures, and the world's past.
  • Medicine: Treating wounds, diagnosing illnesses, and stabilizing the injured.
  • Mysticism: Knowledge of magic, the arcane, the divine, and supernatural phenomena.
  • Perception: Noticing your surroundings, spotting hidden things, and hearing faint sounds.
  • Persuasion: Convincing others through reason, diplomacy, and charm.
  • Society: Knowledge of politics, laws, customs, and social structures.
  • Stealth: Moving silently, hiding, and staying unnoticed.
  • Survival: Tracking, foraging, navigating, and enduring the wilderness.
  • Thievery: Picking locks, disarming traps, and picking pockets.

Levels

The system has 12 levels, divided into 4 equal tiers. As you level up, you gain:

  • Levels 1, 4, 7, and 10: A class feature.
  • Levels 2, 5, 8, and 11: A specialization feature.
  • Levels 3, 6, 9, and 12: +1 to two attributes.

Experience

Experience (XP) is what you need to level up. The XP required to reach the next level is that level × 100. Your XP resets to 0 when you level up, but any excess carries over.

Life

Life measures how much punishment you can take before you go down. When your Life reaches 0, you gain the Fainted condition and 1 Wound. Each additional instance of damage you take while at 0 Life gives you another Wound (or 2 Wounds if it's a critical hit).

To get back up, you must either recover at least 1 Life or succeed on a recovery roll: roll a d20, and on an 11 or higher, you stand back up with 1 Life and lose the Fainted condition.

You can have up to 3 Wounds. If you receive a 4th, you die.

Chapter 3 — Races

Race

Races are groups that share similar traits, although their physical traits are not necessarily the same.

Language

Languages are forms of communication. Within each language there are dialects. Creatures that speak dialects of the same language can understand each other.

Dialect Language
Bretonic Common
Common Common
Dwarvish Dwarf
Elvish Elf
Gnomish Dwarf

Size

Size is how much space a creature occupies on the grid. If a creature tries to move through a space one Size smaller than itself:

  • It is Prone and can only move using Crawl.
  • It has Disadvantage on Attack Rolls and on Strength and Agility Tests.
  • Melee Attacks against it have Advantage, while Ranged Attacks against it have Disadvantage.

Size also affects how much you can carry and your reach. The default is a base Load of 10 and a reach of 1 Zone (for Compact and Standard creatures). Each Size larger adds +5 to your base Load and +1 Zone to your reach. Each Size smaller reduces your base Load by 5, but doesn't change your reach.

Race List

Hill Dwarf

Origin: Hill Dwarves live in the hills that lie between the plains and the mountains. Their settlements sit along the old trade routes, where caravans from the lowlands meet the mines and forges of the peaks. This position makes them natural middlemen: they guard the roads, trade with every people that passes through, and still travel up into the mountains to work the stone and ore. Generations of this life have made them practical, hardy, and sharp when it comes to the worth of things.

Description: Hill Dwarves are less stout than other dwarves, with a lighter, less dense build. Their beards and mustaches are of ordinary length, often kept neat for the sake of business. Their skin ranges from pale to tanned, and their hair and eyes come in a wide variety of colors.

Size: Compact
Height: 1.20–1.50 m
Weight: 50–70 kg
Lifespan: They reach maturity at around 25 years old and live approximately 250–300 years.
Speed: 5 Zones
Languages: Common, Dwarvish

Features

  • Hill Toughness: Your maximum Life increases by 2.
  • Trade Roads: You have Advantage on Tests to appraise the value of Items and to haggle over prices.

Arcane Elf

Origin: Arcane Elves bend the mana of the universe to their will in every aspect of their lives. They weave it into their buildings, their tools, their clothing, and even into their own bodies. To an Arcane Elf, magic is not a discipline to be studied apart from daily life, but the material from which that life is built, and many of them can feel mana as easily as others feel the wind.

Description: Arcane Elves are tall and slender, with pale white skin. Their silky hair ranges from black to white, and their eyes are shades of purple or violet. Their veins carry a faint purple glow and are slightly more visible than other people's, as if mana flowed through them instead of blood.

Size: Standard
Height: 1.70–1.80 m
Weight: 40–60 kg
Lifespan: They reach maturity at around 40 years old and usually live between 450–550 years.
Speed: 6 Zones
Languages: Common, Elvish

Features

  • Mana Sense: You sense the mana that flows through all things. You have Blindsight within 3 Zones.
  • Arcane Blood: When you take damage from a Spell, you can use your Reaction to gain Resistance to that damage. You can use this feature twice per Rest.

Farmland Gnome

Origin: Farmland Gnomes are tireless workers who tend their crops and livestock. They wake before dawn and work until the sun goes down, and their villages are known for rich harvests and well-kept herds. They take pride in honest labor and in knowing every animal on their land by name, and they rarely sleep longer than they need to.

Description: Farmland Gnomes are small and sturdy, with slightly large noses. Their skin is tanned by the sun, their hair is dry and comes in shades of blond, black, and similar tones, and their eyes are blue, green, or amber.

Size: Compact
Height: 0.80–1.25 m
Weight: 20–50 kg
Lifespan: They reach maturity at around 20 years old and usually live between 350–400 years.
Speed: 5 Zones
Languages: Common, Gnomish

Features

  • Early Riser: Your Rest takes 6 hours instead of 8.
  • Way with Animals: You have Advantage on Tests to calm, handle, or understand animals.

Bretonic Human

Origin: Bretonic Humans are driven to conquer, to expand their territory, and to spread their culture, whatever means it takes. Their kingdoms grow through war, trade, diplomacy, and settlement alike, and few peoples have spread as far or as fast. This ambition makes them adaptable: wherever they go, they learn what they need to thrive there.

Description: Bretonic Humans have predominantly fair skin, and they vary widely in build, facial hair, eye color, and hair color.

Size: Standard
Height: 1.60–1.85 m
Weight: 50–100 kg
Lifespan: They reach maturity at around 18 years old and usually live between 90–100 years.
Speed: 6 Zones
Languages: Bretonic, Common

Features

  • Driven to Conquer: Once per Rest, you can give yourself Advantage on a Test or attack.
  • Adaptable: You become Trained in one Skill of your choice.

Forest Orc

Origin: Forest Orcs live deep in dense forests, using the land itself for protection and isolation. They prefer to live among their own tribe, but they are neither hostile nor unfriendly toward outsiders, and travelers who respect the forest are often welcomed. Life among the trees has taught them to move quickly and quietly, to hunt by stealth, and to vanish into the undergrowth when danger comes.

Description: Forest Orcs have skin in shades of green and a lean build rather than a bulky, muscular one. Their hair is black and wavy or curly, and their eyes are yellow or amber.

Size: Standard
Height: 1.70–2.00 m
Weight: 65–90 kg
Lifespan: They reach maturity at around 15 years old and usually live between 70–80 years.
Speed: 6 Zones
Languages: Orcish, Common

Features

  • Forest Stalker: You have Advantage on Stealth Tests in forests, and dense forest isn't Difficult Terrain for you.
  • Swift Runner: Once per combat, you can take the Dash action as a Minor Action.

Chapter 4 — Classes

Class

Your class is one of the main choices you make, and it shapes how you play your character. Each class has its own style of gameplay and follows a broad, unique theme.

Features that improve with your Tier use the notation Tier 1 > Tier 2 > Tier 3 > Tier 4. A feature gained after Tier 1 only lists values starting from the Tier in which you gain it.

Specialization

A Specialization is a specific branch of your class's development. While your class is broad, your Specialization is more focused. You choose your Specialization at level 2.

Role

Even when it isn't stated outright, every Specialization has a Role: whether it is a tank, a damage dealer, or a support, along with the smaller variations within each.

Class List

Archer

Origin: Archers are specialists in long-range weapons. They think in terms of positioning and safety, always looking for high ground, open lines of sight, and a safe distance from danger. Hunters, scouts, and soldiers alike take up the bow, and a good Archer can end a fight before the enemy ever reaches them. Even so, they still carry a blade, and they know how to use it when there's no other choice.

Skills: Choose 3 Skills. Life: Medium.

Starting Equipment:

  • 1 Adventurer's Kit
  • d12 × 10 Aurel
  • Leather Armor
  • Choose 1:
    • Shortbow, 20 Arrows, a Quiver, and 1 Dagger
    • Longbow and 20 Arrows
    • Crossbow, 20 Bolts, and a Quiver
Level Feature
1 Last Resort
4 Double Shot
7 Point Blank
10 Eagle Eye

Class Features

Last Resort (Level 1): When a creature within your Melee Reach attacks you, you can use your Reaction to make a melee Weapon attack against it. You can use this feature 1 > 2 > 3 > 4 times per combat.

Double Shot (Level 4): Your Secondary Attack can be made with your ranged Weapon, dealing full damage.

Point Blank (Level 7): Your ranged attacks no longer have Disadvantage against targets within 1 Zone. In addition, if you haven't moved during your Turn, your ranged attacks have +1 to hit until the end of that Turn.

Eagle Eye (Level 10): Your ranged attacks no longer have Disadvantage when the target is beyond the first range of your Weapon, and they ignore Partial Cover.

Specialization — Deadshot

Deadshots pick a single target and bring it down as quickly as possible before moving on to the next. They are patient and cold, studying their prey for the one shot that ends the fight.

Mark Prey (Level 2): As a Minor Action, you mark a creature you can see. Your Weapon attacks against the marked creature deal +1 > +2 > +3 > +4 damage. Only one creature can be marked at a time, and the mark lasts until the end of the combat. When the marked creature reaches 0 Life, you can mark a new creature for free.

Relentless Aim (Level 5): When you miss your marked creature, you can reroll the attack. You can use this feature 1 > 2 > 3 times per combat.

Deadeye (Level 8): Your attacks against your marked creature score a Critical Hit on a natural 19 or 20.

Killing Shot (Level 11): Once per combat, when you hit your marked creature, you can turn the hit into a Critical Hit.

Healer

Origin: Healers go onto the battlefield to aid and support their allies, keeping them on their feet when the fighting is at its worst. Some are trained physicians, others are self-taught village menders or battlefield volunteers, but all of them share the same instinct: to run toward the wounded while everyone else runs away.

Skills: Choose 3 Skills. Life: Medium. Mana: Caster. Conjuring Attribute: Intellect. Spells: You learn 4 Healer Spells at levels 1, 4, 7, and 10.

Starting Equipment:

  • 1 Adventurer's Kit
  • d12 × 10 Aurel
  • Hide Armor
  • 1 Weapon of your choice
Level Feature
1 First Aid
4 Mender's Step
7 Potent Healing
10 Second Chance

Class Features

First Aid (Level 1): As a Minor Action, you touch a Fainted creature. It regains 1 Life. You can use this feature 1 > 2 > 3 > 4 times per combat.

Mender's Step (Level 4): After you heal a creature, you can move up to 2 Zones without provoking Opportunity Attacks.

Potent Healing (Level 7): When you heal a creature with a Spell, add your Intellect to the amount healed.

Second Chance (Level 10): Once per Recover, when a creature within 6 Zones of you would gain a Wound, you can use your Reaction to prevent it. That creature also regains Medium (d12) Life.

Specialization — Field Doctor

Field Doctors focus on speed. They cross the battlefield at a run, weaving between enemies to reach a fallen ally, and they would rather drag a friend out of danger than wait for the danger to pass.

Fleet Medic (Level 2): Your Speed increases by 1 Zone, and moving through an ally's Zone doesn't cost you extra movement.

Combat Medic (Level 5): When an ally within 6 Zones of you is Injured or Fainted, you can take the Dash action as a Minor Action, as long as you move toward that ally.

Evacuation (Level 8): When you move, you can carry a willing or Fainted creature within 1 Zone of you along with you, and it ends its movement within 1 Zone of you. Neither of you provokes Opportunity Attacks during this movement.

Never Too Late (Level 11): Once per combat, when an ally within 6 Zones of you drops to 0 Life, you can use your Reaction to move up to your Speed toward them. If you end within 1 Zone of them, you can use First Aid on them as part of the same Reaction.

Mage

Origin: Mages manipulate mana directly, without channeling or focusing it. Where other casters shape magic with care, a Mage tears it out of the world and hurls it, and the results are destructive and unrefined. Their magic is too wild to be held steady, so Mages can never Conjure Concentration Spells.

Skills: Choose 3 Skills. Life: Fragile. Mana: Caster. Conjuring Attribute: Intellect. Spells: You learn 4 Mage Spells at levels 1, 4, 7, and 10. You can't Conjure Concentration Spells.

Starting Equipment:

  • 1 Adventurer's Kit
  • d12 × 10 Aurel
  • Robes
  • 1 Dagger
Level Feature
1 Overcharge
4 Destructive Mind
7 Blood Casting
10 Cataclysm

Class Features

Overcharge (Level 1): When you Conjure a Spell that deals damage, you can raise its damage category by one. If you do, you take Weak (d4 > d6 > d8 > d10) Arcane damage, which can't be reduced in any way.

Destructive Mind (Level 4): When you Conjure a Spell that deals damage, you can add your Intellect to the damage dealt to one of its targets. You can use this feature 1 > 2 > 3 times per combat.

Blood Casting (Level 7): You can pay part or all of a Spell's MP cost with your Life instead, losing 2 Life for each MP you don't pay.

Cataclysm (Level 10): Once per Recover, when you Conjure a Spell that deals damage, it deals the maximum possible damage. Afterwards, you become Stunned until the end of your next Turn.

Specialization — Arcanist

Arcanists focus on Arcane damage and Arcane effects. They work with mana in its purest form, untouched by fire, frost, or lightning, and their spells unravel the magic of anything they strike.

Mana Disruption (Level 2): When a creature takes Arcane damage from one of your Spells, it can't take Reactions until the start of its next Turn.

Arcane Focus (Level 5): Your Spells that deal Arcane damage ignore Resistance to Arcane damage.

Arcane Echo (Level 8): Once per combat, after you Conjure a Spell that deals Arcane damage, you can Conjure it again as a Minor Action, paying its MP cost as normal.

Pure Mana (Level 11): When you use Overcharge on a Spell that deals Arcane damage, you don't take damage.

Thief

Origin: Thieves are survivors of the cities, often turning to wrongdoing to feed themselves, to survive, and sometimes out of simple malice. They learn early that a fair fight is a fight you might lose, so they strike fast, chain their blows together, and disappear before anyone can strike back.

Skills: Choose 4 Skills. Life: Weak. Resource: Combo.

Starting Equipment:

  • 1 Adventurer's Kit
  • d12 × 10 Aurel
  • Gambeson
  • 2 Daggers
Level Feature
1 Combo
4 Escape Artist
7 Flow
10 Master Thief

Class Features

Combo (Level 1): Each time you hit a creature with an attack, you gain 1 Combo. You can hold up to 3 > 4 > 5 > 6 Combo, and you lose all of your Combo at the end of the combat. You spend Combo on Finishers, which are features marked with a Combo cost. You start with one Finisher:

  • Eviscerate (Finisher — any amount of Combo): When you hit with an attack, you can spend any amount of Combo. The attack deals +2 damage for each Combo spent.

Escape Artist (Level 4): You gain two Finishers:

  • Slip Away (Finisher — 1 Combo): As part of your movement, you take the Disengage action.
  • Vanish (Finisher — 2 Combo): As a Minor Action, you make a Test to Hide, even while you are being observed.

Flow (Level 7): You start each combat with 1 Combo, and you gain 2 Combo instead of 1 when you score a Critical Hit.

Master Thief (Level 10, Finisher — 3 Combo): Once per Turn, you take an additional Main Action. It can't be the Attack action.

Specialization — Street Scoundrel

Street Scoundrels specialize in dirty fighting. Sand in the eyes, a kick to the knee, a blade in the back: they use whatever works, and they are at their most dangerous when their enemy is already struggling.

In this Specialization, a hindered creature is one that has any Condition other than Concentrating, Hidden, or Injured.

Dirty Trick (Level 2, Finisher — 2 Combo): When you hit a creature, you can throw sand in its eyes or sweep its legs. The creature must make an Agility Test against a Difficulty of 10 + your Agility. On a failure, it becomes Blinded until the end of its next Turn or falls Prone (your choice).

Kick Them While They're Down (Level 5): Add your Agility to the damage of your attacks against hindered creatures.

Low Blow (Level 8): When you hit a hindered creature, you gain 1 additional Combo.

No Honor Among Thieves (Level 11): Once per combat, when a creature within your Melee Reach attacks one of your allies, you can use your Reaction to make an attack against it. If you hit, you can use Dirty Trick without spending Combo.

Warrior

Origin: Warriors are disciplined and trained in many combat techniques, learning to deal with every kind of enemy. Soldiers, mercenaries, knights, and pit fighters all walk this path, and no two Warriors fight quite alike: each one masters their own set of Techniques over a lifetime of battle.

Skills: Choose 3 Skills. Life: Strong.

Starting Equipment:

  • 1 Adventurer's Kit
  • d12 × 10 Aurel
  • Scale Armor and a Heater Shield
  • 2 Weapons of your choice

Class Features

Technique (Levels 1, 4, 7, and 10): You learn one Technique from the list below. You can't learn the same Technique twice, and some Techniques require a minimum level.

Techniques

  • Brawler: Your Unarmed Attacks deal Medium Blunt damage.
  • Dual Wielding: Your Secondary Attack with a Light Weapon deals full damage instead of half.
  • Dueling: While you wield a one-handed Weapon and no other Weapon, you have +1 to hit with it.
  • Extra Attack (requires level 4): Your Secondary Attack can be made with your main Weapon, dealing full damage.
  • Great Weapon Fighting: While you wield a Two-Handed Weapon, add your Strength to its damage once per Turn.
  • Opportunist: Your Opportunity Attacks have Advantage, and when one hits, the target's Speed becomes 0 until the end of its Turn.
  • Protection: While you have a shield equipped, when a creature attacks an ally within 1 Zone of you, you can use your Reaction to give that ally +2 Defense against that attack.
  • Second Wind: Once per Rest, you can use a Minor Action to regain Strong (d8 > d10 > d12 > 3d4) Life.
  • Shield Fighting: While you have a shield equipped, when a creature makes a melee attack against you, you can use your Reaction to gain +2 Defense against that attack.
  • Thrown Weapon Fighting: When you throw a Thrown Weapon, drawing it is part of that same attack, so you don't need to Interact to draw it. In addition, the ranges of your Thrown Weapons increase by 2 Zones.
  • Veteran (requires level 7): Once per combat, you can reroll an attack or Test you failed.

Specialization — Defender

Defenders specialize in protecting their allies and taking damage in their place. They plant themselves at the front of the line, draw the enemy's attention, and refuse to fall while anyone behind them still needs protecting.

Guardian (Level 2): When a creature within 1 Zone of you is hit by an attack, you can use your Reaction to take the hit instead. You become the target of the attack, and it hits you.

Taunt (Level 5): As a Minor Action, you challenge a creature within 6 Zones of you. Until the end of its next Turn, it has Disadvantage on attacks against creatures other than you. You can use this feature 1 > 2 > 3 times per combat.

Bulwark (Level 8): Allies within 1 Zone of you gain +1 Defense.

Last Stand (Level 11): Once per Recover, when you drop to 0 Life, you drop to 1 Life instead, and you gain Resistance to all damage until the end of your next Turn.

Chapter 5 — Equipment

Money

Aurel is the currency of TTE. It is used in most transactions, although other forms of payment and bartering exist. Wealth is measured in goods, not just in coins.

500 Aurel count as 1 Load.

Price Reference

To give a sense of how much things are worth:

Expense Cost
A simple meal 2 Aurel
A night at an inn 5 Aurel
A laborer's daily wage 10 Aurel
A skilled artisan's daily wage 20 Aurel
A riding horse 500 Aurel
A small house in town 5,000 Aurel

Items

Items are objects that can be used or equipped to serve a purpose. Some Items only have an effect while they are equipped.

Equipping or unequipping an Item uses an Interact Action if it is the second object you interact with during your Turn. Armor is the exception: putting it on or taking it off takes 30 minutes.

The Item categories are:

  1. Adventure Gear
  2. Ammunition
  3. Armor
  4. Container
  5. Kit
  6. Potion
  7. Shield
  8. Weapon

Every Item has:

  • Name
  • Category: One of the Item categories.
  • Load: How difficult the Item is to carry.
  • Value: The Item's value in Aurel.
  • Description: What the Item is and what it does.

Containers

Containers hold your Items. A container you wear, like a Backpack, raises your Load limit while you wear it. A container you don't carry, like a Chest or a Barrel, has a capacity: the amount of Load it can hold.

Small Items

Items with a Load of 0 are small enough that one alone doesn't weigh you down. However, every 10 small Items you carry together count as 1 Load. Ammunition is sold in bundles of 20, and each bundle counts as a single small Item, not 20.

Potions

A potion's effect is based on the Tier of the creature that drinks it. Drinking a potion uses an Interact Action. Life potions use the Life table, and Mana potions use the Mana table:

Strong d8 > d10 > d12 > 3d4 d10 > d12 > 3d4 > 2d8

Chapter 6 — Magic

Mana

Mana is the energy that flows through the universe. It is present everywhere, but only a few are able to draw it out or use it correctly.

Magic

Magic is one of the ways of channeling mana, and it can take many forms. Every Spell has a verbal component, some also have a somatic component, and every Spell has a Tier.

  • Verbal (V): You must be able to speak to Conjure the Spell.
  • Somatic (S): You need a free hand to Conjure the Spell.

Half-Caster 4 6 7 9 10 12 13 15 16 18 19 21

A Spell's cost is equal to the Tier at which you Conjure it: a Spell Conjured at Tier 1 costs 1 MP, and one Conjured at Tier 4 costs 4 MP. When you Conjure a Spell, you choose its Tier, up to your current Tier.

Casters learn 4 class Spells at levels 1, 4, 7, and 10. Half-Casters learn 2 class Spells at the same levels.

Spell Information

Every Spell has the following information:

  • Name: The Spell's name.
  • School: The Spell's school.
  • Action: The type of Action used to Conjure the Spell.
  • Range: The Spell's range (Self, Touch, or a distance in Zones).
  • Duration: How long the Spell lasts and whether it requires Concentration.
  • Description: Everything else you need to know, including what the Spell does, its targets, area, damage, and how it improves with each Tier.

Values that change with the Tier at which the Spell is Conjured use the notation Tier 1 > Tier 2 > Tier 3 > Tier 4.

Spell Areas

  • Line: A straight line extending from one point to another.
  • Square: A square-shaped area that expands equally from its point of origin.
  • Cone: A cone whose narrow end begins at the point of origin and expands outward.
  • Circle: An area that expands equally from its center.

Hitting with Spells

Spells that target a single creature are attacks: you roll d20 + your Conjuring Attribute against the target's Defense. Spells that affect an area usually require their targets to make a Test instead, against the following Difficulty:

10 + your Conjuring Attribute

Unless the Spell says otherwise, a creature that succeeds on this Test takes half damage and doesn't suffer the Spell's Condition.

Schools of Magic

Every Spell belongs to a school, which describes its category:

  • Creation: Creates effects by solidifying mana, from flames and lightning to raw bursts of force.
  • Enchantment: Enchants the mind of the target.
  • Protection: Shields, wards, and barriers that defend against harm.
  • Restoration: Restores Life and cures harmful effects.

Chapter 7 — Exploration

Tests

Tests come up frequently and depend on your approach: how you choose to tackle the challenge. Depending on your approach, a Test can lead to different resolutions, outcomes, and consequences. Depending on the situation, a Test may or may not be retried, with or without changes to the approach.

Attribute Test

d20 + chosen Attribute

Skill Test

d20 + chosen Attribute + Skill

Flat Test

d20

Passive Test

10 + applicable modifiers

For Passive Tests, Advantage and Disadvantage apply a flat +3 or -3.

Opposed Test

Each creature makes the appropriate Test, and their results are compared against one another. If the results are tied, the situation remains unchanged.

Group Test

Every participating creature makes the required Test individually. If at least half of the group succeeds (rounded up), the entire group succeeds. Otherwise, the group fails.

Extended Test

A process resolved through several Tests made in sequence. The Difficulty, type of Test, Attribute, Skill, or other circumstances may change between rolls. It usually requires reaching a certain number of successes before reaching a certain number of failures.

Help

A creature can help another creature perform a Test, granting Advantage on that Test, as long as the assistance can meaningfully contribute to the action. If the help would not reasonably affect the task, it provides no benefit.

Load

Load is how much you can carry, equal to 10 + your Strength. If you exceed it, you can't carry anything else.

Visibility

Visibility depends on the lighting of the environment and on a creature's vision.

Lighting

  • Bright Light: You see without penalty.
  • Dim Light: Tests that rely on sight have Disadvantage.
  • Darkness: You gain the Blinded condition.

Vision

  • Darkvision: Within a certain range, you see darkness as if it were dim light, and dim light as if it were bright light.
  • Blindsight: You don't see with your eyes, but with your other senses. Lighting rules don't affect you.

Cover

Cover is protection you have from one specific direction.

  • Partial Cover: You gain +2 Defense.
  • Total Cover: You can't be hit from that direction.

Nourishment

You need to eat and drink daily. If you do not, you gain 1 Level of Deprived. This is not a strict requirement, and some situations may require you to eat or drink more.

Social

Social interactions represent how you interact with NPCs. NPCs have a Disposition, which determines how difficult it is to convince them during a Test.

  • Opposed: +4
  • Reluctant: +2
  • Normal: 0
  • Receptive: -2
  • Supportive: -4

Stealth

Stealth is compared against Perception. To hide, you make a Stealth Test and compare the result against the target's Passive Perception, which uses Spirit as its Attribute. To find a hidden creature, you make a Perception Test.

Downtime

Downtime is used to recover. To use it, you must have at least 1 Life, and you can't enter Initiative, take damage, Conjure Spells, or perform strenuous activities.

  • Rest: 8 hours of rest. You can only Rest once every 24 hours. You recover half of your maximum Life and Mana.
  • Recover: 24 hours of rest in good conditions. You recover all of your Life and Mana, and remove 1 Wound.

Chapter 8 — Combat

Initiative

Initiative is a d20 roll. If there's a tie, compare Agility. If it's still tied, roll again.

Turn

A Turn lasts 10 seconds. On your Turn, you can take one Movement Action, one Main Action, and one Minor Action. Outside your Turn, you can take one Reaction.

Simple actions, such as speaking or interacting with your first object, are free.

Movement Action

Movement uses a grid of 1.5-meter Zones. Every creature has a Speed that determines how far it can move. Difficult Terrain costs +1 Zone of movement.

Your movement is a single pool. If you have more than one type of Speed (such as a Swim Speed) and switch between them during your Turn, subtract the distance you've already moved from the new Speed. For example, if you walk 2 Zones and then enter water with a Swim Speed of 6, you can swim up to 4 more Zones.

If you end your Turn in the same space as another creature, you gain the Prone condition. Forced Movement doesn't count against your movement.

You can move through another creature's Zone. That Zone is considered Difficult Terrain unless the creature is Minute, an ally, Incapacitated, or at least 2 Sizes larger or smaller than you.

  1. Move: Move a number of Zones up to your Speed.
  2. Drop Prone: You willingly gain the Prone condition.
  3. Crawl: While Prone, you can only move by Crawling. Movement costs +1 Zone, or +2 Zones through Difficult Terrain.
  4. Stand Up: Remove the Prone condition. Standing up costs 2 Zones of movement.
  5. Climb: Climbing costs +1 Zone, or +2 Zones through Difficult Terrain, unless you have a Climb Speed.
  6. Swim: Swimming costs +1 Zone, or +2 Zones through Difficult Terrain, unless you have a Swim Speed.

Main Action

  1. Attack: You make an attack with a Weapon, an Unarmed Attack, or another attack source.
  2. Conjure: You Conjure a Spell.
  3. Dash: You gain additional movement equal to your Speed.
  4. Interact: You interact with a second object. Interacting with one object is free.
  5. Search: You search for something using your Attributes or Skills.
  6. Hide: You make a Test to hide. On a success, you gain the Hidden condition.
  7. Help: You help another creature.
  8. Disengage: You make a careful retreat, preventing Opportunity Attacks.
  9. Dodge: Attacks against you have Disadvantage until your next Turn.
  10. Improvise: If the Action you want to perform doesn't fit any of the others, you can improvise. The Game Master determines the Test, the Difficulty, and whether the Action is possible.

Minor Action

  1. Secondary Attack: You make an attack with your Secondary Weapon.

Reaction

  1. Opportunity Attack: If a creature leaves your Melee Reach without using Disengage, you can make an attack against it. Forced Movement doesn't trigger this attack.

Attack

Attacking means striking with a weapon or unarmed. Spells can also be attacks, but they use the Conjure action instead. An attack is a d20 + Attribute Test against the target's Defense.

Melee attacks have a reach of 1 Zone. Ranged attacks have Disadvantage when the target is within 1 Zone of you.

  • Unarmed Attack: Uses Strength for the attack roll and deals Weak Blunt damage (d4 > d6 > d8 > d10).
  • Secondary Attack: Made with a second equipped weapon that has the Light property.
  • Opportunity Attack: Happens when a target leaves your melee reach without using Disengage.

Damage

Damage is what makes you lose Life. When you score a critical hit, you take the maximum value of the damage dice and roll them again, adding both. Your Attribute is not added to damage.

Damage Types

  1. Blunt: Damage caused by impact, crushing force, or heavy pressure, such as hammers, falls, or powerful strikes.
  2. Cut: Damage caused by edges that slice or tear through a target, such as swords, axes, or claws.
  3. Thrust: Damage caused by a concentrated point piercing a target, such as spears, arrows, or other pointed Weapons.
  4. Blast: Damage caused by explosive force, shockwaves, intense sound, or sudden pressure.
  5. Heat: Damage caused by fire, extreme temperatures, or other sources of intense heat.
  6. Freeze: Damage caused by extreme cold, ice, or effects that rapidly drain heat from the target.
  7. Arcane: Damage caused directly by raw magical energy and supernatural forces.
  8. Lightning: Damage caused by electricity, lightning, or powerful electrical discharges.

Affinity

Affinities represent resistances, weaknesses, and immunities to damage types. They are applied as the last step when calculating damage.

  • Resistance: The damage is halved.
  • Weakness: The damage is doubled.
  • Immunity: The damage is reduced to 0.

Conditions

Conditions are temporary states that affect creatures. They don't stack unless they have Levels.

  1. Blinded: You can't see. You automatically fail all Tests that require sight, you make attacks with Disadvantage, and attacks against you have Advantage.
  2. Deafened: You can't hear. You automatically fail all Tests that require hearing.
  3. Mute: You can't speak, and you can't Conjure Spells.
  4. Burned: You are on fire. At the end of your Turn, if you still have this Condition, you take Medium Heat damage based on the Condition's Tier. To end the Condition early, you must become Prone and roll on the ground. Water, ice, and the Chilled condition also end Burned.
  5. Chilled: You are freezing. At the end of your Turn, you take Weak Freeze damage based on the Condition's Tier, and your Speed is halved. Warming yourself up or gaining the Burned condition ends Chilled.
  6. Deprived: Represents the physical deterioration of your body caused by poor nourishment and other hardships. It has 5 Levels:
    1. Disadvantage on attacks and Conjuring.
    2. Your Speed is halved.
    3. Disadvantage on all Attribute Tests.
    4. Your maximum Life is halved.
    5. Every time you gain another Level of this Condition, you gain 1 Wound.
  7. Fainted: You have lost consciousness, becoming Incapacitated and Prone. You drop everything you're holding, your Speed becomes 0, and you're unaware of your surroundings. Attacks from within 1 Zone automatically hit and are Critical Hits, and you automatically fail Strength, Agility, and Dexterity Tests.
  8. Hidden: You are hidden from a creature. Your attacks against that creature have Advantage, and its attacks against you have Disadvantage. Making noise, being seen, attacking, and other Actions can make you lose this Condition.
  9. Injured: You are Injured while you have half or less of your maximum Life.
  10. Scared: You are afraid of something. You can't willingly move toward the source of your fear, and you roll with Disadvantage while the source of your fear is within your sight.
  11. Incapacitated: You can only take Movement Actions, you are Mute, and you can't take Reactions. You lose Concentration.
  12. Prone: You are lying on the ground and can only move by Crawling. Your attacks have Disadvantage. Attacks against you from within 1 Zone have Advantage, while Ranged Attacks from farther away have Disadvantage. You can use Stand Up to end this Condition.
  13. Restrained: Your Speed becomes 0. Attacks against you have Advantage, and your attacks have Disadvantage.
  14. Concentrating: You are concentrating on a Spell. Its duration depends on the Spell, and it can end in the following ways:
    • You end it voluntarily.
    • You Conjure another Concentration Spell.
    • You take damage. To maintain Concentration, make a Test with your Conjuring Attribute against a Difficulty of 10 or half the damage you took, whichever is higher.
    • You become Incapacitated or die.
  15. Stunned: You are stunned. You are Incapacitated, you automatically fail Strength, Agility, and Dexterity Tests, and attacks against you have Advantage.
